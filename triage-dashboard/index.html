How a packet gets its grade

The grade says how complete and consistent the packet is. It never says whether the patient qualifies. A coordinator always decides.

Step 1: score the 27 facts

  • Found (with a page): 2 points
  • Unclear or Can't tell (a date is blacked out): 1 point
  • Missing: 0 points
  • Redacted (a black box): not counted. A black box is privacy, not a gap.

Score = points ÷ (2 × facts that apply), shown as a percent.

Step 2: pick the grade

GreatAll 7 core documents are there (IHH form, face sheet, authorization, H&P, vitals, medication list, PT/OT or nursing notes).Score 85% or more.No conflicts between the form and the chart.Next step: ready to decide.
NeutralCore documents are there.Score 60% to 84%, or one or more conflicts a phone call can settle.Next step: request specific items.
BadAny critical alert (a rule that would exclude the patient looks true).Or a core document is missing.Or score under 60%.Next step: call the hospital before deciding.

When a packet is incomplete

A Neutral or Bad packet always comes with a to-do list. Each item starts with what to do:

  • Clinical review: a nurse or medical director reads the page before anything else.
  • Get: ask the hospital for a missing document (for example PT/OT notes).
  • Find: the document is there, but the fact isn't. Look again or ask (for example MRSA result).
  • Settle: the form and chart disagree. Check both pages; the chart wins, or call.
  • Confirm with hospital: the rule needs a date we can't see.
  • Coordinator decides: the written rules don't settle it.

When every item is done and the score reaches 85% with no conflicts, the packet becomes Great.

Priority: who to look at first

The grade says how complete a packet is. Priority says how soon to look at it, based on the patient's need. It never decides eligibility.

  • Discharge expected today: +3. Tomorrow: +2.
  • Answer clock late: +3. At risk: +2.
  • Unhoused (would leave to the street): +1.
  • High care need (insulin, IV, oxygen, wound care, isolation): +1.
  • Same problem happened recently (repeat episode): +1.

P1 Urgent is 6 or more points, P2 High is 3 to 5, P3 Routine is 2 or less. Answered packets show Done.

The answer clock

IHH promises hospitals an answer within 2 hours. On time is under 90 minutes, At risk is 90 to 120 minutes, and Late is over 2 hours. Answered packets show Answered.

Alert types

  • Critical: a rule that excludes the patient looks true (for example dementia, suicidal thoughts, aggression, isolation not cleared).
  • Missing document: one of IHH's 10 checklist documents is not in the packet.
  • Missing fact: the document is there but the fact isn't (for example no MRSA result).
  • Contradiction: the form and the chart disagree. The chart wins.
  • Can't tell: the rule needs a date or time, and dates are blacked out.
  • Rule gap: IHH's written rules don't settle it. The coordinator decides.

Staff guide: using the board

For referral coordinators and nurses. Five steps, about 10 minutes a packet.

1. Start with the queue

Work from the top: the queue is sorted by priority (P1 Urgent first). Watch the answer clock. Amber means you have under 30 minutes left on the 2-hour promise.

Click a packet name to open Packet details: summary, key missing information, priority with its points, patient details (name, address, allergies, blood type), the original fax or email as a PDF, the email log, the packet page by page, medications, and medical history with recent episodes.

Log every message in the email log: what arrived, what you sent, and any draft. Times show to the second, with the time since the referral arrived and since the previous message. Live clocks show time since received, since the last message, and how long is left on the 2-hour promise.

To answer a hospital, use Reply to the hospital under the log (or "Reply" on a received message). It starts from the packet's open items. Copy message and send it through IHH's approved channel. The board never sends, and drafts you add to the log last only until you reload.

2. Look at the grade

  • Great: go to the facts and decide.
  • Neutral: send the hospital the short list of items, then keep working.
  • Bad: call the hospital now. Read the critical alerts first.

3. Read the alerts, then the pages

Click the packet to see its alerts. Each one names the page. Open that page and read it yourself. The tool can misread a crooked fax.

4. Tick Verified

On the printable checklist, tick Verified for each fact you checked on the page. The board counts how many facts a person has checked.

5. Decide and write it down

You decide. The tool never approves or denies. Write the reason, especially for a denial.

Keyboard and screen reader

  • Tab moves between controls. The first stop offers "Skip to the queue" and "Skip to alerts".
  • On the section tabs, ← → switch tabs, and Home and End jump to the first or last.
  • In the queue, each packet name is a button. Enter opens its details and moves focus to the packet's title. "Back to the queue" returns you to the same packet.
  • Use "Show alerts for" above the alerts table to filter by packet without a mouse.
  • Changes are announced: which packet is open, its priority, grade and open items.
  • Every status has a word and a symbol, not only a color (✓ good, ! needs work, ✕ blocking, ▲ urgent).

Remember

  • A black box is privacy, not a missing document.
  • "Can't tell" means the rule needs a date we can't see. Ask the hospital if it matters.
  • When the form and the chart disagree, the chart wins.
  • Sex offender status is case by case. Never auto-deny.
  • Never type names or record numbers into notes.

IT guide: how the board works

For whoever sets it up and keeps it running.

What it shows

Two lists: one row per packet (its fact counts, documents, alerts and times) and one row per alert. The board works out the score, grade and answer clock from those rows. Open Sources in the toolbar to see each list and the math behind every number.

Where the data comes from today

Two small sample files with made-up packets. Nothing here comes from a real referral.

How to connect the real tool later

  1. The review tool writes one review.json per packet (see the plan, section 5).
  2. A small script turns those into the two lists, with the same column names as the sample files.
  3. Replace the sample files in Sources, or point the board at a database table with the same columns.

Rules that don't bend

  • No patient details on the board. Packet IDs only. No names, dates of birth, record numbers, or page images.
  • Never connect to IHH's live systems in the prototype.
  • The tool sends nothing. Hospital requests are drafts.
  • Keep packets and page images in a folder that git ignores.
  • API keys go in environment variables, never in code.

Columns the board needs

Packets: packet_id, hospital, received, minutes_open, status, pages, required_docs_present, docs_present, facts_applicable, facts_found, facts_unclear, facts_cant_tell, facts_missing, facts_verified, critical_alerts, contradictions, minutes_to_ready, missing_docs

Alerts: alert_id, packet_id, type, what, rule_id, page

Profiles: packet_id, summary, discharge, housing, reason, care_needs, recent_episode, recent_note, high_need, patient_name, address, allergies, blood_type, fax_pdf

Pages: page_id, packet_id, page_from, page_to, doc_type, key_content

Medications: med_id, packet_id, medicine, dose, route, how_often, self_administers, purpose, page

History: hist_id, packet_id, condition, status, recent, page

Email log: log_id, packet_id, time, direction (Received, Sent, Draft), channel (Fax, Email, Phone), who, subject, status. Staff record what they sent; the board never sends.

Profiles also carry patient_name, address, allergies, blood_type, fax_pdf. In the prototype, name and address must stay "Removed for privacy" for real packets, and real PDFs stay on a staff computer.

The real packets must never be uploaded to a published board. For real packets, run this view as a local file on a staff computer.

Accessibility statement

Target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA. What the board does:

  • Keyboard: every action works without a mouse; tabs follow the ARIA tabs pattern (arrows, Home, End); skip links; focus is never lost when the board redraws; focus moves to the packet title when details open and back to the packet when you return.
  • Screen readers: real headings, tables with captions and column and row headers, a polite live region for changes, charts with text equivalents, PDF links that say they open a new tab.
  • Vision: status uses a word and a symbol plus color; text uses the theme's contrast-checked colors in light and dark; works at 200% zoom and narrow widths with no sideways page scroll; Windows high-contrast mode keeps borders, bars and the selected row visible.
  • Motion and targets: no animation when reduced motion is on; tabs are 44 px tall and other controls at least 24 px (WCAG 2.2 target size).
  • Language and time: page language is set; nothing times out or erases work.

Known gaps: not yet tested with JAWS, NVDA or VoiceOver by real users; the mock PDFs are not tagged for screen readers (real faxes are scanned images, so the page-by-page table is the accessible version); a formal audit is a pilot step.

If something looks wrong

  • A card says "Failed to load": open Sources and check the file is there.
  • A grade looks off: check the "How each packet scored" table. Every number is shown.
