# About Illumination Health + Home (IHH)

Who IHH is, what it does, and how it helps people and Orange County. Each fact names its source, and IHH's own claims are marked as IHH's.

## Who IHH is

- **A nonprofit based in Santa Ana**, known until 2025 as Illumination Foundation. *(event background, `docs/background.md`)*
- **Founded in 2008** as The Illumination Foundation. *(IHH website, home page)*
- **Its mission**, in its own words: "to disrupt the cycle of homelessness by providing healthcare, housing, and hope—together." *(IHH, Mission and Vision page, via `docs/background.md`)*
- **It serves people experiencing homelessness** across Orange County. Its forms also list Los Angeles and the Inland Empire. *(IHH website)*

## What IHH does

IHH pairs **housing** with **healthcare** and **case management**, which it calls "full-circle care". *(IHH website)*

| Program | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Recuperative care** (also called medical respite) | Short-term residential care for people experiencing homelessness who are too ill or frail to recover on the street or in a shelter, but don't need a hospital. It provides a bed, meals and health monitoring: vital signs, wound care, and tracking medications. It is not a hospital or a nursing home. *(National Health Care for the Homeless Council and California DHCS, via `docs/background.md`)* |
| **Children & Families Recuperative Care** | Recovery care for a child, with the family staying together. IHH describes it as the nation's first center of its kind. *(IHH's claim, IHH website)* |
| **Navigation centers and emergency housing** | Safe, temporary places to stay while getting connected to stable housing. *(IHH website)* |
| **Family emergency homes** | Shelter for families, including Plumeria House in Anaheim, for families with a child who has a chronic illness or disability. *(IHH website)* |
| **Permanent supportive housing** | Affordable housing with ongoing services, for people with disabilities and long histories of homelessness. *(IHH website)* |
| **Behavioral health and substance use counseling** | Counseling for behavioral health and substance use. *(IHH website)* |
| **Family programs** | Parent and Me classes, tutoring, "Mini Med School", financial literacy, and help with transportation to medical appointments. *(IHH website, client stories)* |

**Scale:** IHH's 2024 Impact Report counts **7,282 clients served, 2,287 of them in recuperative care**. *(via `docs/background.md`)*

IHH's website shows different, undated "last year" figures (6,826 served, 2,329 in recuperative care), and a 2025 figure of 1,719 people housed. Say which source you're quoting.

## How it fits into the health system

- **The law:** California law requires hospitals to plan discharge for patients who are homeless and to find a destination, giving priority to a sheltered one with supportive services. The hospital must send the receiving provider information about the patient's care needs. That information is the referral packet. *(Health and Safety Code 1262.5, via `docs/background.md`)*
- **Who pays:** Medi-Cal health plans can pay for recuperative care as a "Community Support." In Orange County that plan is CalOptima Health:
  - stays are authorized 30 days at a time
  - up to 90 consecutive days
  - each renewal needs the client's care plan

  *(CalOptima Health provider guide, January 2026, via `docs/background.md`)*
- **The promise:** IHH tells hospitals it will answer a referral within **2 hours**. *(IHH referral form RC0725)*

## How IHH benefits people and the community

| Benefit | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **A safe place to heal instead of the street.** People leaving the hospital get a bed, meals and health monitoring while they recover. | What recuperative care provides *(NHCHC, DHCS)* |
| **Fewer days back in the hospital.** In a Chicago study of 225 homeless hospital patients, those discharged to respite care spent **3.7** hospital days over the next year, against **8.3** for usual care. | Buchanan et al., 2006 |
| **Fewer readmissions.** A review of 13 studies found medical respite "reduced future hospital admissions, inpatient days, and hospital readmissions." Results for emergency visits and cost were mixed but promising. | Doran et al., 2013 |
| **Lower public cost.** California's Medicaid agency found recuperative care associated with a **29.2%** net cost reduction (preliminary analysis). | DHCS fact sheet, October 2025 update |
| **Hospitals can discharge safely and on time**, meeting their legal duty to find a sheltered destination, which frees hospital beds. | Health and Safety Code 1262.5 |
| **Families stay together** while a child recovers, and families get shelter built for children with medical needs. | IHH programs *(IHH website)* |
| **A path from crisis to housing.** Navigation centers, case management and supportive housing connect recovery to a stable home. | IHH programs *(IHH website)*; IHH reports 1,719 people housed in 2025 *(IHH's figure)* |

## Why it matters in Orange County

- Orange County counted **6,321** people experiencing homelessness in January 2026, and **3,065** of them were unsheltered. *(County of Orange)*
- Among unsheltered adults, about **a third** reported a physical disability and about **a third** a serious mental illness. *(2026 Point in Time Count report)*

These are the people most likely to land in a hospital and have nowhere safe to recover.

## How Anchor helps IHH help people

Hospitals refer patients to recuperative care, and IHH promises an answer within 2 hours.

- **Faster, surer answers.** The verification checklist, rule overlay and triage board help coordinators find the deciding facts in long faxed packets. They show what's missing and look first at the patients who need it most.
- **Fewer patients discharged with nowhere to go.** The intake pipeline keeps hospitals informed at every step. Red flags reach the clinical reviewer right away, and the hospital as soon as a clinician confirms, so questions are answered before the clock runs out.
- **More time for people, less for pages.** Coordinators spend less time hunting through paper and more with patients and hospital partners.

Anchor is a prototype built by Team Beacon. A coordinator makes every decision.
